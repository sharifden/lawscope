    Our commitment in brief

    We publish educational information—not legal advice. We prioritize authoritative sources, identify jurisdiction and dates, separate verified facts from commentary, correct material errors, and keep editorial decisions independent from advertising.

    Mission, Audience, and Scope

    Lawscope serves people seeking a clearer starting point for understanding U.S. law. Our guides explain legal vocabulary, rights, responsibilities, institutions, and common processes for a general audience. The publication does not provide legal advice, predict outcomes, create attorney-client relationships, or evaluate a reader’s individual facts, strategy, or deadlines.

    Article Selection and Public Interest

    We select subjects that address recurring public questions, explain significant legal processes, or add useful context to material legal developments. Search interest may help us understand reader needs, but no page is published solely to target a keyword or manufacture authority. We do not promise comprehensive coverage of every jurisdiction or legal issue.

    Primary Sources and Citation Standards

    Whenever practical, research begins with primary legal materials and official public guidance: constitutions, statutes, regulations, court opinions and orders, rules, agency materials, and government publications. Reliable secondary sources may help explain context or identify primary authority, but they do not replace controlling law where controlling law is available.

    Published guides identify sources that support material statements. Citations use a descriptive label and destination, and include publisher, publication date, or access date when relevant. Editors check that a cited source supports the statement made and that links resolve at the time of review. A link is not an endorsement, and external materials can move or change after publication.

    Plain-English Writing and Jurisdiction

    We define necessary legal terms, favor direct language, and organize material for scanning and careful reading. Plain-English editing must not erase exceptions, procedural posture, uncertainty, or limits that affect meaning. Headlines and summaries must accurately reflect the article and must not promise a result.

    Guides identify whether they concern federal, state, local, tribal, or territorial law. We state when rules vary by jurisdiction and avoid presenting one state’s rule as nationally applicable. State-specific coverage is published only when it can be accurately researched and maintained. Readers are reminded that law and official guidance can change.

    Authorship, Contributors, and Reviewer Identification

    Launch articles are published under the organizational byline The GetLawscope Team. We do not invent people, biographies, credentials, or review claims. If an individual contributor or reviewer is introduced, Lawscope will verify the person’s identity and relevant credentials before publication and will clearly identify the role performed. A reviewer credit will not imply authorship or endorsement beyond the disclosed scope.

    Legal Review

    Lawscope does not represent launch articles as attorney-reviewed. If qualified legal review is commissioned in the future, the page will identify the reviewer, relevant jurisdiction, review date, and scope of review only after those facts are verified. Legal review supports editorial quality; it does not turn general information into advice to a reader and does not create an attorney-client relationship.

    Fact-Checking, Dates, and Updates

    Before publication, editors check the article’s claims against its sources, citation destinations, jurisdiction labels, dates, names of institutions, procedural posture, and required disclaimer. Publication dates show when an article first became public. A “last updated” date appears only after a substantive change or documented review, not for routine formatting changes.

    Time-sensitive guides are reviewed as practical when a cited authority changes or a reliable report raises a material question. Updates preserve important qualifications and replace or annotate outdated sources. The most recent review cannot guarantee that every later legal development has already been incorporated.

    Corrections Process

    Readers may report a possible factual error, outdated authority, broken citation, or misleading passage through the Contact page. Please include the article URL, the statement at issue, and an authoritative source when available.

    1. We record the report without treating it as a request for personal legal analysis.
    2. An editor checks the published statement and the most relevant available sources.
    3. We correct verified errors as promptly as practical and update affected metadata.
    4. Material corrections receive a visible note explaining what changed; minor spelling or formatting fixes may be corrected without a note.

    Legal-News Standards

    Legal-news coverage is clearly dated and distinguishes allegations from proven facts, interim orders from final judgments, procedural steps from merits decisions, and a court’s holding from separate concurrence, dissent, party argument, or commentary. Headlines avoid declaring guilt, liability, or finality that the underlying record does not establish. Developing coverage is updated when the legal posture materially changes and identifies what remains unresolved.

    Conflicts, Commercial Relationships, and Editorial Independence

    Editors and contributors must disclose interests that could reasonably affect their work. Gifts, affiliate relationships, sponsorships, and paid placements may not be concealed. Sponsored content, if ever accepted, must be distinctly labeled and must not imitate independent editorial coverage.

    Advertisers do not select, assign, write, edit, review, approve, or suppress Lawscope editorial content. Advertising status does not affect corrections or conclusions. This Editorial Policy and other trust and legal pages remain free of advertising.

    AI-Assisted Work

    AI tools may assist approved back-office tasks such as organizing notes, suggesting non-substantive edits, or checking consistent formatting. A responsible human editor must verify all sources, citations, claims, dates, jurisdictions, and final wording before publication. AI output is never presented as legal advice, and fabricated or unverified citations are prohibited.

    Confidential user material, private personal facts, unpublished contact submissions, and secrets are not entered into public AI tools. AI assistance does not replace the human accountability attached to the published organizational byline.

    User Submissions and Privacy

    Suggestions and correction reports may inform coverage, but submission does not create an attorney-client relationship or promise publication, individual advice, or a response. Users should not send confidential legal facts, secrets, court deadlines, or emergency requests. Contact information and message content are handled according to the Privacy Policy and are not committed to the public content repository.

    Accessibility and Inclusive Language

    Lawscope aims to make content perceivable, operable, understandable, and robust. We use semantic structure, descriptive alternatives for meaningful images, keyboard access, visible focus, readable contrast, and plain instructions. Inclusive language should be accurate, respectful, and consistent with the terminology people and legal sources use, while quotations and official titles are preserved when context requires. Accessibility concerns are welcomed through the Contact page.

    Contact and Policy Changes

    Questions about this policy, corrections, accessibility, or editorial practice may be sent through the Contact page. Lawscope cannot use that channel to provide legal advice or evaluate a personal matter.
